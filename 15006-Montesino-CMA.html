Your two strongest selling points

The roof and air conditioner. Buyers shopping homes built in 2006 and 2007 are actively screening for roof age, and so are their insurance carriers. A five year old roof carries 15 to 20 years of remaining service life and clears underwriting without the questions an original roof invites. Nothing else in this price range offers that right now.

Condition and availability. With new flooring, fresh paint, and the house empty, your home shows as finished and move-in ready. Both homes that sold quickly in this analysis were easy to show and ready to occupy. That combination is what produces offers in the first weeks rather than the second month.

Why the price difference is justified

That listing is marketed to investors, describing the home as income-producing with tenants in place and ready for the buyer's personal touches. Investors buy at a discount, and the showing requirements of 24 hour notice through an out of area agent mean most weekend buyers never get inside. After 38 days it has not found a contract.

Your home reaches a different buyer entirely. It is finished, vacant, available immediately, and carries a roof and air conditioner that a 2007 home with original systems cannot match. The $24,900 difference between the two prices reflects real, documented differences in condition and systems, not optimism.

One thing this means for our marketing

Because both homes will appear side by side in search results, the listing photos and the opening lines of the description have to communicate the difference immediately. The new flooring, the fresh paint, and the 2021 roof and air conditioner will lead the presentation, so that a buyer comparing the two online understands what the higher price buys before they ever schedule a showing.

What to expect

Priced at $439,900 and listed before the end of November, your home should go under contract in 25 to 45 days and close 35 to 40 days after that. From listing to the closing table is realistically 60 to 85 days, which places a late November launch at a late January or early February closing.

A home listed at $429,900 would likely contract in 12 to 25 days. A home listed at $449,900 would likely take more than 60 days and require a reduction.

What slow starts have cost other sellers here

15612 Montesino launched at $605,000, spent nine months on the market, and closed at $560,000. 1034 Bella Vida launched at $598,000, reduced in stages to $529,000, and was eventually withdrawn without selling. In both cases the market was not the problem. The opening price was, and the cost of correcting it was far greater than the amount the sellers were originally reaching for.
